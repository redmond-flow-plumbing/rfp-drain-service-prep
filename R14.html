How to Prepare for Drain Service: A Homeowner's Checklist

You've booked the visit — the tech is coming tomorrow morning for that slow kitchen drain. What you do tonight determines whether it's a quick fix or a long appointment. Knowing how to prepare for drain service saves you time, saves the tech time, and sometimes saves you money. Here's our field-tested checklist.

Clear the Workspace Before We Arrive

This is the single biggest time-saver, and almost nobody does it. Empty the cabinet under the sink completely — cleaning products, trash bags, the whole archaeology of under-sink life. We need room to work, see, and set tools down, and every minute spent moving your bleach bottles is a minute you're paying for. Same goes for the area around a floor drain or cleanout: move boxes, bins, and laundry out of the way. If the problem is a tub or shower drain, clear the tub itself and make sure we can reach the access panel, usually on the wall behind the tub or in an adjacent closet. For a main-line issue, locate your sewer cleanout — usually a capped pipe near the foundation or in the yard — and make sure it's accessible, not buried under mulch or a parked car.

Do NOT Pour Chemicals Down the Drain

We say this on every call and we'll say it here: skip the caustic drain cleaner. Those bottles promise miracles and deliver two things — a drain that's still clogged, and a pipe full of hazardous chemical soup that our tech now has to work around. Caustic cleaners can burn skin and eyes, damage older pipes, and they rarely dissolve the actual obstruction (grease, roots, and solidified buildup laugh at them). Enzyme-based products are gentler but still won't clear a real blockage. The best thing you can do before our visit is nothing: leave the drain alone so we can diagnose it cleanly. If you've already poured something, tell us exactly what and when — for our safety.

Write Down the Symptoms

Drains tell stories, and the details matter. Before the visit, note: which fixtures are affected (just the kitchen sink, or the bathroom too?), when it started, and whether it's getting worse. Does the toilet gurgle when the washer drains? Does water back up into the tub when you run the sink? Those cross-fixture symptoms tell us whether we're dealing with a branch line or the main sewer — completely different jobs with different equipment. Note any odors, any history of this drain clogging before, and whether anyone recently put something unusual down it (the toddler-and-the-toy-car incident is more common than you'd think, and there's no judgment here).

Questions Worth Asking Your Tech

While we're there, get your money's worth from the expertise. Good questions: What's actually causing this — and will it come back? Should I be worried about the rest of the line, or is this isolated? Would a camera inspection be worth it here? What can I do to prevent this specific clog? And the practical ones: Is this covered under any warranty on the work? What do I do if it backs up again next week? A good tech welcomes these questions — they show you're paying attention, and the answers help you avoid paying for the same visit twice. At Redmond Flow Plumbing, we'd rather explain the "why" than just clear the "what."

What Happens During the Visit

Knowing the flow takes the mystery out. Our tech will start by asking about your symptoms and the history you noted — this is where your prep pays off. Then comes diagnosis: for a fixture drain, that usually means checking the trap and running a cable; for deeper or recurring issues, we may recommend a camera inspection so you're looking at the actual problem on a screen, not taking our word for it. You'll get a clear explanation of what we found, your options (sometimes there's a quick fix and a thorough fix — we'll price both), and flat pricing before any work starts. After the repair, we test every affected fixture, clean up the work area, and walk you through what we did and how to keep it from recurring. The whole visit for a standard clog typically runs under ninety minutes.
